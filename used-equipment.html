Used buying

Used tool & equipment checklist

Inspect the actual item instead of buying the story in the listing.

Before meeting

  • Get the exact model number.
  • Compare current new and used pricing.
  • Confirm batteries, chargers, guards, cases and accessories.

Inspect

  • Check housings for cracks and impact damage.
  • Check cords, plugs, switches and battery contacts.
  • Look for missing safety components.
  • Run the tool if possible.
  • Check bearings, slides, wheels, latches and adjustments.

Price repair risk

A cheap tool needing a major repair may not be cheap. Include missing parts and likely repairs in your offer.

Rule: If you cannot test a used powered tool, price it as if repair may be required—or walk away.
